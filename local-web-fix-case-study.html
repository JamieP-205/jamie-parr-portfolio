Case study

Local Web Fix

A small service website I built around a simple idea: help local businesses fix practical website and online-information problems without pretending every job needs a full redesign.

Why I built it

A lot of small business sites have simple problems that matter more than a flashy rebuild: opening hours are hard to find, contact buttons do not work well on mobile, booking links are buried, or information differs between the website and other listings.

I wanted to build a small service around fixing those kinds of issues and make the scope obvious from the start.

What I focused on

The site is deliberately straightforward. It explains what the free check is, what paid work can include, what I will not promise, and how someone can contact me without handing over passwords or unnecessary access.

The work was mostly about structure, wording and trust rather than complex code. I kept the layout mobile-first and made the pricing, limits and safety guidance visible instead of hiding them behind marketing copy.

What the project shows

It gave me practice taking an idea from nothing to a live domain, making product decisions myself and thinking about how a non-technical person reads a service page. It also forced me to keep the implementation small instead of adding features that did not improve the actual offer.

What I would improve next

The biggest gap is evidence from real jobs. The site can explain the service clearly, but a real before-and-after example would do more than another paragraph of copy. That is the next thing I would add once I have permission to publish one.
